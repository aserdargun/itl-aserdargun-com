# Twin Capsule

A Twin Registry identifies and locates digital machine representations across an asset hierarchy:

```text
Enterprise → Fleet → Plant → System → Machine → Component
```

Each machine can own a **Twin Capsule**: a bounded, versioned record of identity, engineering metadata, sensor map, operating envelope, physics model, failure library, historical data, maintenance history, feature catalogue, experiment history, model registry, safety constraints, uncertainty, provenance, and documentation. The capsule is not the twin runtime itself. It is the traceable contract that tells a runtime, experiment, and reviewer exactly which representation was used.

The Phase 1 P-101 fixture populates only the typed records rendered below. Historical data, maintenance history, experiment history, model registry, and documentation remain conceptual categories; no record is claimed where none exists. Physics-model availability, limitations, and uncertainty are explicit in the capsule ledger.

## P-101 anatomy

P-101 is a fictional centrifugal boiler feed water pump driven by an electric motor. Its capsule is `{P101_TWIN.version}` and its asset hierarchy is `{P101_TWIN.asset.hierarchy.join(" → ")}`. The fixture defines eleven signals and six failure modes; those same identifiers are reused throughout this publication.

| Record               | P-101 example                                                                                      | Why it matters                                          |
| -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Identity             | P-101, centrifugal pump, electric-motor drive                                                      | Prevents results from drifting to an ambiguous asset.   |
| Engineering metadata | 240 m³/h design flow; 112 m design head                                                            | Anchors interpretation in units and design intent.      |
| Sensor map           | pressure, flow, power, speed, bearing temperature, vibration, ambient temperature                  | Connects features to physical locations and quantities. |
| Operating envelope   | bounded flow, pressure, and ambient ranges                                                         | Defines where evidence may be applicable.               |
| Failure library      | bearing and impeller degradation, cavitation, suction restriction, seal leakage, motor degradation | States the mechanisms under investigation.              |
| Safety constraints   | read-only fixture; no setpoint authority; human review                                             | Keeps the representation outside control authority.     |
| Provenance           | `{P101_TWIN.provenance.assetVersion}` and `{P101_TWIN.version}`                                    | Makes later experiments reproducible.                   |

At the illustrative nominal flow of 240 m³/h, suction pressure of 2.6 bar and discharge pressure of 13.6 bar imply about 73.3 kW of hydraulic power. With 101 kW of electrical input, the implied wire-to-water efficiency is about 73%. The 112 m design head uses a reference water density of 1,000 kg/m³, equal inlet/outlet elevation and velocity heads, and rounded values. These assumptions make the teaching record internally consistent; they do not validate a boiler-feed application or define a pump curve. The energy accounting follows the <a href="https://www1.eere.energy.gov/manufacturing/tech_assistance/pdfs/pump.pdf" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">U.S. Department of Energy pumping sourcebook<span className="sr-only"> (opens in a new tab)</span></a>.

### Declare the model’s purpose

A Twin Capsule should not imply a perfect replica. A model adequate for testing a pressure-ratio feature may be inadequate for bearing-frequency diagnosis. Every capsule therefore declares its operating envelope, assumptions, uncertainty, and validation status. Changes to signal calibration, physics equations, failure mechanisms, or safety constraints require a new version; silently mutating a twin would invalidate prior Evidence Packages.

The registry makes assets discoverable, while the capsule makes their context reviewable. Later phases may allow capsule editing or API-backed storage, but the stable contract remains: an experiment references a specific asset and twin version rather than whichever data happens to be current.
